    How the scores and the ICT check work
    • Pair bias: bullish means the last H4 close is above the EMA and the EMA is rising; bearish is the reverse. Ranging and neutral pairs score 0.
    • Currency score: a bullish pair gives its base currency +1 and its quote currency −1. A bearish pair does the opposite. Each currency ends between −7 and +7.
    • Best pair: the pair whose base and quote scores are furthest apart. A positive gap is a buy, a negative gap is a sell.
    • Liquidity sweep: within the last 48 candles, price wicks beyond the previous New York day's high or low (or, if you untick the box, a recent swing point) against the bias, then closes back inside.
    • USD strength: the USD score from the 28-pair scan stands in for the dollar index. It is a check on USD pairs and context on the rest.
    • Trade plan: shown for every pair you check. With a confirmed or forming setup, entry is the middle of the fair value gap and the stop is one pip beyond the sweep. Without one, it uses the current price and your fixed stop distance. The target is your reward for every 1 of risk (2 means 1:2), and the plan shows stop and target in pips.
    • Position size: balance × risk % ÷ (stop distance in pips × pip value), with the pip value converted to USD from the latest majors. A stop wider than 20 pips is flagged.
    • Strong-pair alarm: after each scan, any pair whose gap reaches your threshold sounds an alarm once, and again only if it drops below the threshold and comes back.
    • Structure shift: after the sweep, a candle closes beyond the last swing point in the direction of the bias.
    • Fair value gap: a three-candle gap in the bias direction, with a large middle candle. It becomes the entry zone.
    • Retrace: price has pulled back into that gap. Premium or discount and the London or New York killzone are shown as context.
    • Check all 28 pairs: one tap downloads the seven majors, then the 21 crosses in three batches of 7, one batch per minute (about 3 minutes, 28 requests). Results appear after the first batch, with the crosses derived from the majors, and update as each batch lands.
    • Quick check: downloads only the seven majors (7 requests) and derives the crosses from them.
    • Watching: each check downloads one pair's 15-minute candles (1 request per pair). Three pairs checked every 15 minutes use about 288 requests a day, and a re-scan after each H4 close adds about 168, against the free plan's 800.

    Your API key stays in this browser and is sent only to api.twelvedata.com. The free plan allows 8 requests a minute and 800 a day. Mobile browsers often pause background tabs, so watching works best on a computer or with the screen kept on.
